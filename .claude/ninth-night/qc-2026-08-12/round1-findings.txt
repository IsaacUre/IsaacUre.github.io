######## PERFORMANCE AND MEMORY OF THE WORLD DRAW PATH
NOTES: Allocation churn in draw() is a non-issue and I can prove it: with --enable-precise-memory-info, 1200 warmed frames grew the heap by 793 bytes/frame in the square, 2167 on the road, 1859 in the arena. That is 46-127 KB/s at 60fps, well inside one young-generation scavenge per minute. Note the arena, which has zero props and zero NPCs, allocates more than the square, which has ten props and three NPCs: the ents array, its per-entity closures, the forEach and sort comparators, RT.hide, lightsOf's fresh array, and interactables()' per-frame objects and string concatenations are not where the garbage is.
buildFloor's cache-hit path is a key string concat, one property lookup and touchFloor's indexOf/splice/push over at most 7 strings. Unmeasurably small; the equivalent operation at 10x the scale (touchSprite for ten props against a 58-entry LRU) measured 0.006 ms/frame.
propSprite's cache-hit path is the same shape and is covered by that same 0.006 ms measurement. The sprite LRU never exceeds 64 entries.
The spriteCtx fallback path lasts exactly two frames. On a truly cold entry to the square (close() frees both caches, then walk in): frame 1 has 0 sprites resident, frame 2 has 7, frame 3 has 9 and it is done. Two frames of plain solids, about 33 ms. The first frame builds nothing at all because the floor blit consumes the 5 ms budget before the first prop is reached, which is the right outcome by accident.
performance.now() from mayBuild() is evaluated eagerly as an argument, so it is paid once per prop per frame even when every sprite is cached. Ten calls measured 0.001 ms. Not worth changing.
close() leaks nothing. 20 open/close cycles with 160 frames each: heap 3.07 MB before, 3.07 MB after (delta 0.01 MB), DOM canvases 1 throughout. freeFloors and freeSprites both zero cv.width/cv.height rather than just dropping references, and both clear their LRU arrays and the byte counter.
SPR (the baked figure cache, comp/ninth.js:1840) is the one canvas holder close() does not free, but it does not grow: it settled at exactly 20 entries and stayed there across all 20 cycles. Every bake() key is drawn from a fixed set (pc+S/W/P, foe.<kind>, npc.<id>+S/W/P, prop.<k>.<id>, folk<coat>.<skin><S|U>) and the figures are ~32x32, so it is roughly 80 KB total.
I checked drawFolk's bake key (comp/ninth.js:3472) for the classic under-specified-cache-key bug, because the palette's hair term uses i%3 while the key only encodes i%6 and (i>>1)%4. It is safe: i%6 determines i%3, so the key does cover the palette. Every other bake() call site has a constant or key-derived palette.
SHADE (comp/ninth.js:6591) is bounded and never grows with play time. Its key is hex+'|'+round(k*64); the hex side is bounded by the palette tables (7 FLOOR_PAL x 3, 28 PROP x 3, plus about 15 literals in the material painters, ~120 distinct) and every k in the file is a constant or a constant plus a bounded rng, the widest range being [0.62, 1.24], i.e. 40 quantised steps. Worst case ~4,800 entries at roughly 90 bytes, under 0.5 MB, and in practice far less because each hex only meets one or two k formulas. It survives close() by design, as a pure-function memo should.
No O(props^2) and no O(foes*props). coversSomeone loops RT.hide, which is assigned a fresh one-element array at comp/ninth.js:3882 and written nowhere else in the file (grep gives three sites: the assignment and two reads), so it is O(props). blocked() is O(props) per moving actor, which is at most about 312 AABB tests a frame with twelve actors and thirteen props. Measured: arena 0 to 12 spawned foes went 3.01 to 3.62 ms/frame, loft (six props) 0 to 12 foes went 3.74 to 3.73 ms/frame.
Entering a place costs three hot frames and then settles: square 23.7, 11.0, 6.3 then 4.2 ms; a3sq 19.7, 9.3, 3.9; road 11.6, 7.3, 4.9. The first frame is the deferred rasterisation of the newly built floor bitmap landing on the drawImage. It happens under bigLine's place title and is one to two dropped frames in software raster. Worth knowing, not worth fixing, and about half of the residual 4.2 ms steady figure is finding 1.
trimSprites cannot evict the sprite drawProp is about to blit: propSprite touches the new key to the LRU tail before calling trimSprites, which shifts from the head, and the SPRITE_LRU.length > 1 guard covers the degenerate case. Within a frame, each prop is drawn before the next is built, so a build can never invalidate a canvas already handed out.
